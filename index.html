Which cabin type is actually right for you?

Every cruise line uses its own brand names, but almost all cabins fall into four basic types. Here's what each one actually means, plus the premium enclaves worth knowing about and the terms you'll see in reviews.

The four cabin types

Interior

$

No window — fully enclosed, usually toward the middle of the ship. Some newer ships add a screen showing a live outside view ("virtual balcony").

Typically 120–185 sq ft

Oceanview

$$

A real window or porthole, but no door — you can't step outside. Natural light without balcony prices. Check for an obstructed view before booking.

Typically 150–200 sq ft

Balcony

$$$

A private outdoor space, usually with two chairs and a small table. The most popular category for a reason — fresh air and a view of every port.

Typically 175–250 sq ft, plus balcony

Suite

$$$$

Significantly more space, often a separate bedroom or lounge, and a bigger balcony. Perks vary hugely by line — a "suite" isn't the same experience everywhere.

Typically 300 sq ft and up

Terms worth knowing

Midship
The middle of the ship — generally the most stable, with the least motion.
Forward / Aft
Toward the front (forward, or bow) or back (aft, or stern) of the ship.
Muster station
Your assigned safety assembly point, listed on your cabin door and key card.
Obstructed view
A window or balcony partly blocked, often by a lifeboat or the ship's own structure.
Guarantee cabin
You book a category without picking an exact cabin — the line assigns one before sailing, sometimes as a free upgrade.
Connecting cabins
Two cabins with an internal connecting door — useful for families or groups travelling together.

Cunard, explained

New to Cunard? Here's what makes it different from a typical cruise line, and exactly what each cabin grade actually gets you.

New to Cunard?

Cunard is a British line with over 180 years of history, and it sails differently to most modern cruise lines — think formal nights, ballroom dancing and a proper afternoon tea, delivered under Cunard's long-standing "White Star Service" style of hospitality. Its fleet of four ships — Queen Mary 2, Queen Victoria, Queen Elizabeth and Queen Anne — sail worldwide, including Queen Mary 2's regular transatlantic crossings. Queen Mary 2 is, in fact, the only true ocean liner still in service today, built to cross open ocean rather than just hop between ports.

Cabins split into two broad tiers. Britannia is the standard experience open to every guest, covering Inside, Oceanview and Balcony cabins. The Grills sit above that as Cunard's top tier, each with its own restaurant, lounge and outdoor deck. Britannia Club sits in between — a Balcony cabin with a service upgrade, without stepping all the way up to a Grill suite.

Every Cunard cabin grade

Britannia Inside

Britannia

$

No window, usually toward the ship's interior. The simplest, most affordable way to sail with Cunard.

Included: Full access to the Britannia Restaurant and every standard onboard venue — the same ship experience as every other Britannia guest.

Britannia Oceanview

Britannia

$$

A real window or porthole, but no door to step outside. Natural light without the balcony price jump.

Included: Same as Britannia Inside, plus a view out to sea.

Britannia Balcony

Britannia

$$$

A private balcony with outdoor seating — the most popular way to experience Cunard's classic ocean-liner style.

Included: Same Britannia-tier dining and service as Inside/Oceanview, plus private outdoor space.

Britannia Club Balcony

Britannia Club

$$$$

A balcony cabin with an elevated service package on top — not a suite, but treated a step above standard Britannia.

Included: Priority embarkation and disembarkation, and reserved seating in a dedicated Britannia Club dining area with a more attentive service style.

Princess Grill Suite

Grills

$$$$$

The entry point to Cunard's top accommodation tier — a true suite with a separate seating area.

Included: Dining in the exclusive Princess Grill restaurant, access to the Grills Lounge and the private Grills Terrace sun deck, priority boarding and disembarkation, and a noticeably higher staff-to-guest service ratio than Britannia.

Queens Grill Suite

Grills

$$$$$$

Cunard's top tier — the largest suites on the ship, with the most personalised service Cunard offers.

Included: Dining in the most exclusive Queens Grill restaurant, the Queens Grill Lounge, access to the Grills Terrace, service that can extend to a butler on the largest suites, and top priority for embarkation, disembarkation and shore excursions.

Exact inclusions — drinks packages, gratuities, specific perks — can change between ships and sailings. Confirm current details with Cunard or your travel agent before booking; this page describes the general grade structure, not a specific sailing's pricing.
