Cabin guide

Which cabin type is actually right for you?

Every cruise line uses its own brand names, but almost all cabins fall into four basic types. Here's what each one actually means, plus the premium enclaves worth knowing about and the terms you'll see in reviews.

The four cabin types

Interior

$

No window — fully enclosed, usually toward the middle of the ship. Some newer ships add a screen showing a live outside view ("virtual balcony").

Typically 120–185 sq ft

Oceanview

$$

A real window or porthole, but no door — you can't step outside. Natural light without balcony prices. Check for an obstructed view before booking.

Typically 150–200 sq ft

Balcony

$$$

A private outdoor space, usually with two chairs and a small table. The most popular category for a reason — fresh air and a view of every port.

Typically 175–250 sq ft, plus balcony

Suite

$$$$

Significantly more space, often a separate bedroom or lounge, and a bigger balcony. Perks vary hugely by line — a "suite" isn't the same experience everywhere.

Typically 300 sq ft and up

Terms worth knowing

Midship
The middle of the ship — generally the most stable, with the least motion.
Forward / Aft
Toward the front (forward, or bow) or back (aft, or stern) of the ship.
Muster station
Your assigned safety assembly point, listed on your cabin door and key card.
Obstructed view
A window or balcony partly blocked, often by a lifeboat or the ship's own structure.
Guarantee cabin
You book a category without picking an exact cabin — the line assigns one before sailing, sometimes as a free upgrade.
Connecting cabins
Two cabins with an internal connecting door — useful for families or groups travelling together.
